A page about one measurable thing

Grey text is how a site quietly becomes unreadable.

Nobody sets out to publish text at 2.85:1. It happens one tasteful grey at a time, in a design that looks fine on the laptop of whoever chose it. Contrast is the one part of colour that isn’t taste — it’s arithmetic, and it can be checked.

Check a pair See where it goes wrong

01The rule

A contrast ratio compares the relative luminance of two colours: sRGB channels are linearised, weighted 0.2126 red, 0.7152 green, 0.0722 blue, and the ratio is (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05). Two identical colours give 1:1. Black on white gives 21:1, which is the most that exists.

WCAG 2.1 then asks for different minimums depending on what the colour is doing. The thresholds that matter:

WCAG 2.1 contrast minimums. AA is the usual legal and practical bar; AAA is stricter.
What it is AA AAA
Normal text — under 24px, or under 18.66px when bold 4.5:1 7:1
Large text — 24px and up, or 18.66px and up when bold 3:1 4.5:1
Non-text — control borders, focus rings, icons, graphical objects 3:1 —

Two things about that table get missed. Large text is a much narrower category than people hope: 24px is not a heading, it’s a big heading. And non-text is where the quiet failures live, because a border that is invisible as a boundary still passes a visual review — nothing looks broken.

03How to fix it

Move lightness, not hue. Contrast is a difference in luminance. Swapping a blue accent for a purple one at the same lightness changes nothing a contrast ratio can see. Darkening it by ten percent does.

Go away from the background, not past it. On a light background, text gets darker. If you lighten light text instead, you spend the entire budget crossing the background’s luminance and arrive at 1.2:1.

Suspect the tint, not the text. The classic failure is accent-coloured text on a 10% tint of that same accent: the hue is identical, so the only thing keeping them apart is a lightness difference nobody checked. If a pair must stay on-brand, darken the text version of the accent — #a1440e on the tint #f7e8dc measures 5.24:1, and #86380e on the same tint measures 6.78:1.

Flip the label when the fill is pale. A pale accent can be a beautiful button, as long as the text on it is dark. That is what the last two rows above show: same fill, one label colour apart, 1.76:1 versus 10.50:1.

Give up the flourish, not the reader. 4.5:1 is a floor, not an aspiration. If a colour cannot get there without looking wrong to you, the colour is wrong.

04Worked example: this site

I wrote this site’s palette by eye first, then measured it. Everything passed except one thing I had not thought of as a colour at all: control borders.

--border-strong started at #c4bcae, a warm grey that looked right against paper. Against the page background it measured 1.8:1 — well under the 3:1 that non-text UI needs, and invisible to anyone with low contrast sensitivity. The fix was to walk it darker until it passed: #8f887b, 3.37:1 against the page background. In the dark palette the same fix moved #3b4551 to #646e7c — 3.69:1 against the page, 3.41:1 against raised surfaces.

That is the entire audit, across both palettes: 13 pairs each, 26 measurements, no failures. The numbers are on the palette page, and the script that produces them ships in the repository next to the stylesheet it reads.
